Why Example Widgets is betting on brand again

By Casey Placeholder

Synthetic article for evaluation. Example Widgets Inc. is a fictional company.

For three years Example Widgets put nearly all of its marketing budget into performance ads: search and social ads judged by the sales they produce within days. This year it is moving 25% of the budget into brand advertising, which aims to make people remember and prefer the company long before they are ready to buy.

The warning sign

The company's share of voice, its portion of all advertising seen in the widget category, fell from 18% to 11% as two rivals spent heavily. Over the same period, the cost of each search click rose 40%, because fewer people searched for the brand by name and the company had to bid for generic terms instead.

How they will measure it

Brand effects are slow and hard to tie to a single sale. The team will run brand lift studies: survey a group that saw the ads and a similar group that did not, and compare how many in each group recognise and would consider the brand. A lift of 5 points in consideration is the goal for the first six months.

The risk

Sales may dip in the short term, because money leaves channels that convert quickly. The chief marketing officer has agreed with finance to accept up to a 3% drop in quarterly leads while the brand spend builds.
