The Quiet Engine of Wealth

Compound interest rewards time more than money. A simple chart shows why starting early matters so much.

What compounding is

With simple interest you earn a return only on the money you put in. With compound interest you also earn a return on past returns. Each year's gains join the base that next year's gains are calculated on, so growth keeps speeding up.

The difference between simple and compound interest is small in year one and enormous in year forty.

See it grow

Why time beats timing

Growth is exponential, so the last years add the most. At 7%, money doubles about every ten years (the rule of 72: 72 ÷ 7 ≈ 10). Someone who starts ten years earlier gets one more doubling, and that doubling happens at the end, when the balance is largest.

Money That Makes Babies

Why saving early is like planting a tree.

The seed

Imagine you plant one apple seed. It grows into a tree, and the tree drops apples. Each apple has seeds, and you plant those too. Soon you don't have one tree. You have a forest, and you only planted one seed.

Money works the same way

When you save money, the bank gives you a little extra money each year. Next year, that extra money earns extra money too. It's like the apples growing new trees.

Why early matters

The forest grows fastest at the end. If you plant your seed a few years earlier, you get a much bigger forest, not just a slightly bigger one.
